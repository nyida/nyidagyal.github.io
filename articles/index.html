Formal state representation and transition semantics
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Foundations

Build a precise model of the board, ordered body, food, and legal transitions. Write the transition function before choosing the planner. Body order and update order are part of the problem.

A* search: admissibility, consistency, and state design
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Algorithms
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Understand admissibility, tie-breaking, and the gap between efficient search and safe play. A heuristic is useful only relative to explicit movement rules and a correctly implemented search state.

Hamiltonian cycle invariants for collision avoidance
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Algorithms
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Why a board-spanning cycle can prevent self-collision, and exactly when the argument stops applying. A Hamiltonian policy is powerful because its safety follows from an invariant. Its assumptions belong beside the result.

Space-time planning with endogenous occupancy
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Algorithms
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Move from static cells to timed states without losing the snake’s history. Time helps only when the state also preserves the history needed to predict future occupancy.

Value-based and policy-gradient methods for Snake
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Research
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Build a learning problem whose observations, actions, rewards, and evaluation correspond to the game you mean. The neural network is only one component. Observation semantics, episode boundaries, and evaluation determine what learning success means.

Potential-based reward shaping and policy invariance
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Research
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Distinguish useful training signals from incentives that reward the wrong behavior. Calculate the incentives on short trajectories. When using potential shaping, preserve its discount and boundary assumptions in code.

Experimental design for reproducible Snake evaluation
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Research
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Specify rules, randomness, failure metrics, and uncertainty before declaring one controller better. A credible comparison is a recoverable experiment: rules, code, randomness, raw outcomes, and uncertainty travel together.

Observation sufficiency and the Markov property
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Research
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Body order, pending growth, and hidden state determine whether an observation supports reliable decisions. Before increasing model capacity, verify that the observation retains the information the decision requires.

Tail release, growth, and collision constraints
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Foundations
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Understand vacating cells, growth, and the off-by-one errors that change legal moves. A cell is not simply occupied or empty: its availability depends on transition timing and growth.

Boundary conditions and wall-mode topology
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Foundations
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Separate boundary walls, fixed obstacles, and walls that appear during play. Wall geometry, body timing, and wall-generation rules must be specified independently.

Reachable-space heuristics and their limitations
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Algorithms
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Use flood fill as a diagnostic for confinement—and understand what it cannot certify. Reachable area measures a static component. Survival depends on how the body can move through it.

Articulation points and dynamic escape routes
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Algorithms
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Read the board through cut vertices and the timing of a return path. A bottleneck marks a dependence on one passage. Whether it becomes fatal is a question about time and body order.

State-space growth and computational complexity
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Foundations
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Distinguish graph size, body configurations, planning horizons, and hardness claims. Complexity belongs to a precisely defined problem. A small visual board can encode many ordered-body states.

Deterministic simulation and reproducible transitions

Separate transitions from rendering, make randomness explicit, and test the semantics. A reproducible engine is a specification you can execute. Rendering should not decide the rules.

Translate “leave yourself room” into explicit assumptions, measurements, and failure cases. A strategy becomes testable when its objective, state features, action rule, and failure criterion are explicit.

Compare classic Snake, wrapping boards, wall variants, and simultaneous multiplayer. Changing one rule can invalidate a heuristic, a proof, or a benchmark comparison.

Breadth-first search on static occupancy graphs
SnakeGuide
Technical note, 2026 · Algorithms
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Find a shortest food route, then examine what that route fails to prove. BFS certifies a shortest path in the graph you give it. It does not certify the future safety of the snake.

Weighted shortest paths and risk-sensitive objectives

Technical note, 2026 · Algorithms
article/interactive figure/references/bibtex

Use weighted paths without confusing a penalty function with a survival probability. Dijkstra makes a weighted objective precise. The credibility of the resulting move still depends on the objective and state representation.
